How to Review What Your AI Engineering Team Did — Trends View & Review Dashboard (2026)

August 2026 — Trends view, Review dashboard, Unblock everything, Fleet org chart

Quick answer: amux gives you three team-level review instruments — the Trends view (groups every prompt you sent into themes with per-theme status counts), the Review dashboard (weekly synthesis of tasks, tokens, per-epic card counts, and blockers across all sessions), and Unblock everything (a single-screen triage of every card waiting for your input). All three update live, work from your phone, and require no manual reporting from any agent.

The weekly team review problem with AI agents

Once you're running more than 3–4 AI coding agents in parallel, the question "what did my team accomplish this week?" becomes genuinely hard to answer. Each agent has its own terminal, its own board cards, its own token spend. Reading each session's output individually takes 20–30 minutes. Asking each agent to self-report produces duplicated, inconsistently formatted summaries that you still have to synthesize yourself.

Traditional project management tools (Linear, Jira, Notion) don't know about your AI agents at all — you'd have to manually update tickets based on what the agents did, which defeats the purpose of running agents in the first place.

amux solves this at the infrastructure layer. The board, message history, and session status are the shared source of truth for the entire fleet. The review instruments — Trends view, Review dashboard, Fleet org chart — read from that shared state directly, so there's nothing to sync, nothing to manually update, and nothing to ask an agent to summarize for you.

Trends view: see what your AI team actually worked on

The Trends view is in the Messages tab of the amux dashboard. It answers a specific question that per-session message logs can't: what were you actually trying to accomplish across your whole AI team?

How it works

Every time you send a message to an agent — assigning a task, steering a session, asking for an update — that message lands in the board and message history. The Trends view groups these messages into themes automatically, then shows per-theme status counts:

  • Theme label — the common thread across a set of related tasks ("authentication", "database migrations", "API performance")
  • Queued — how many tasks in this theme are waiting to be picked up
  • Doing — how many are in progress right now
  • Done — how many have been completed (awaiting verification)
  • Needs you — how many are blocked on a human decision

Expanding any theme row shows the individual board cards and messages that belong to it, each with a direct link to the session or card. This means you can verify that "done" means actually done — not just reported as done — by clicking through to the card's history or the session's terminal.

What it replaces

Before Trends, reviewing the week meant: reading each session's peek panel individually, checking each board card manually, or asking every agent to write a summary (which took tokens and produced inconsistent results). Trends collapses that into a two-level view: themes at the top, evidence under each theme. A full week of work for a 10-agent team becomes readable in under 5 minutes.

The "needs you" column

The most important column in the Trends view is "Needs you." Any task that's blocked on a human decision appears here immediately when the agent marks the card as needs-you or when its status transitions to that state. A zero in the "Needs you" column across all themes means your AI team is unblocked and self-sufficient. Any non-zero count means there's a specific decision or approval that only you can unblock — and clicking through takes you directly to the card or session.

Review dashboard: the weekly synthesis

The Review tab in the amux dashboard shows a weekly synthesis across your entire fleet — without any prompting, without asking any agent to generate a report, and without leaving the dashboard.

What the weekly synthesis covers

The Review dashboard reads from the board and message history over a rolling 7-day window and shows:

  • Total tasks worked — how many board cards moved through the queue this week
  • Total tokens spent — fleet-wide token consumption with an estimated dollar cost
  • Per-epic breakdown — for each active project or epic, how many cards are in todo / doing / done / verified status. This is the team-level answer to "how is Project X going?"
  • Blockers section — a live list of every card currently in needs-you status, ordered by how long they've been waiting

No manual reporting required

The Review dashboard is the opposite of asking agents to write standup summaries. Agents don't generate the review — the review is derived from the board state that agents update as they work. A card marked done is done. A card in needs-you status appears in blockers. The synthesis is deterministic and auditable: you can trace every number in the Review dashboard to the board cards that produced it.

Reading the per-epic table

The per-epic breakdown is the highest-signal part of the weekly synthesis for engineering managers and CTOs. Each row represents a project or epic (grouped by card tags or the first segment of the session name). The columns show the count of cards in each status. A healthy epic has mostly "done" and "verified" counts; a struggling epic has a large "doing" count with low "done" — agents are working but not finishing. An epic with "needs you" counts needs your attention today.

Unblock everything: one-screen blocker triage

'Unblock everything' is a Focus mode accessible from the Review tab. It's designed for a specific workflow: you've looked at the weekly synthesis, you see cards blocked on you, and you want to work through them efficiently without jumping between sessions.

How it works

Clicking 'Unblock everything' surfaces every card in needs-you status across all sessions into a single scrollable list. For each blocked card you see:

  • The card title and the session that created it
  • The blocker context — what the agent recorded when it set the card to needs-you
  • A Nudge button that sends a message to the waiting session directly from the triage interface, without navigating away
  • A link to open the full session peek if you need more context

The Nudge action

The Nudge button is the critical piece. Without it, unblocking an agent requires: finding the card, reading the context, navigating to the session, opening the send bar, typing a response. With Nudge, the workflow is: read the context, type your decision or clarification, send — next card. A morning triage of 8 blocked cards takes 5–10 minutes instead of 25.

When to use it

'Unblock everything' is most valuable first thing in the morning after an overnight run. Agents working through the night will flag anything they can't resolve themselves. Opening the Review tab, clicking 'Unblock everything', and clearing the needs-you queue is how you start the day as an AI engineering team lead: clear the blockers first, then let the fleet run.

Fleet org chart: your AI team at a glance

The Graph tab in the amux dashboard has a Notes / Fleet switch at the top. The Notes view shows your Obsidian vault graph. The Fleet view shows your live AI agent team as an org chart.

What it shows

Each running session appears as a node, grouped by department. Departments are derived from the leading segment of the session name — so sessions named backend-api, backend-auth, and backend-migrations all group under "backend," while frontend-home and frontend-checkout group under "frontend." The grouping is visual only: it doesn't affect how agents work or how they coordinate.

Each node shows:

  • The session name
  • A status indicator — active (green), waiting (amber), or stopped
  • The current board card the session is working on (if any)

Clicking any node opens that session's peek panel directly, without navigating to the main session list first.

The color palette

Department colors are assigned from a validated 8-slot palette that passes contrast, chroma, and color-vision-deficiency checks — adjacent departments have colors that are distinguishable even under protanopia and deuteranopia. Colors are assigned in sorted department order, so they're stable across restarts. If you have more than 8 departments, colors cycle (but the department label still uniquely identifies each group).

Layout persistence

The fleet org chart saves node positions when you drag them. The layout persists across refreshes and between browser sessions — you can arrange the chart to reflect your team's actual structure (frontend on the left, backend on the right, infra at the bottom) and it stays that way. The labels and status dots remain live even with a pinned layout.

The 10-minute morning review routine

When running a parallel AI engineering team overnight, the morning review is the most important 10 minutes of the day. Here's the routine that works at any team size:

  1. Open the Review tab (30 seconds) — scan the weekly totals and per-epic breakdown. How many cards moved? Which epics are healthy vs. stuck?
  2. Click 'Unblock everything' (3–5 minutes) — triage every card in needs-you status. Use Nudge to send decisions directly. Clear the queue before agents start the new day.
  3. Switch to the Fleet view in the Graph tab (1 minute) — check that all expected sessions are running. Any session that's stopped when it shouldn't be needs a manual restart.
  4. Open the Messages tab → Trends view (2–3 minutes) — scan the theme breakdown for anything unexpected. A theme that should be done by now but has high "doing" counts needs investigation.
  5. Send any steering messages (2 minutes) — for sessions that need new direction, send them directly from the fleet view or the session list.

This routine works from your phone. All five steps are fully functional in the amux iOS app and mobile PWA — the Review tab, Trends view, 'Unblock everything' focus mode, and Fleet org chart all render correctly at 375px with touch-target-compliant buttons.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see what my AI agents accomplished this week?

Open the Review tab in the amux dashboard. It shows a weekly synthesis across all sessions: total tasks, total tokens, per-epic card breakdown, and a live blockers list. Generated automatically from the board — no prompting required.

What is the amux Trends view?

The Trends view is in the Messages tab. It groups every prompt you sent to your AI team into themes — one row per theme with counts of queued, doing, done, and needs-you cards. Expanding any theme shows the individual board cards and messages with direct links.

How do I see which AI agents need my input?

Two ways: the "Needs you" column in the Trends view shows per-theme blocked counts; the 'Unblock everything' Focus mode in the Review tab surfaces all needs-you cards in one list with a Nudge button to respond directly.

Can I see a live org chart of my AI agent team?

Yes — in the Graph tab, switch from "Notes" to "Fleet." Sessions appear grouped by their name prefix (e.g., backend-*, frontend-*) with live status dots and current card labels. Click any node to open that session's peek panel.

Does the weekly review require any manual input from agents?

No. The Review dashboard reads from the board and message history that agents update as they work. If an agent marks a card done, it appears in the synthesis. No agent needs to write a summary or standup report for the weekly view to be accurate.

Does this work with Claude Code, Codex, and Gemini CLI together?

Yes. amux is runtime-agnostic. The board, Trends view, Review dashboard, and Fleet org chart work identically for Claude Code, Codex CLI, and Gemini CLI sessions — and for mixed teams running multiple runtimes simultaneously.

How do I get started with amux for team management?

Install amux (curl -fsSL https://amux.io/install.sh | bash), register your project directories with amux register, and start sessions with amux start session-name. The dashboard runs at https://localhost:8824. The Review tab, Trends view, and Fleet org chart are always available — they populate as you assign work to your agents. See the getting started guide or request a managed setup from the amux team.
